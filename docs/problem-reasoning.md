# PO evidence review for supplier invoices

Buckinghamshire Healthcare NHS Trust reports **275 PO lines raised after invoice date** in June 2026 and AP follow-up with departments. It says identifying the responsible department can delay payment; unrecorded receipts and disputes also block approval. This establishes a recurring handoff, without ranking its burden. [June finance report, PDF pp15–16](https://www.buckshealthcare.nhs.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/11.2-M03-Finance-Report.pdf#page=15).

AP would use the current cohort queue to choose a review action. The AP manager would use the arrival diagnostic to investigate upstream conditions. The immediate decision is: **which PO header evidence needs investigation, and which department role should review it?** Assumed stakeholders: AP manager, proposed process owner, wants timely approvals within staffing limits; AP processor wants actionable evidence but cannot authorise purchases; departmental buyer/budget holder wants resolution within delegated authority; supplier wants predictable payment but cannot correct internal records. These goals and constraints are hypotheses, not interviews.

Reference workflow: requisition → authorised order → supply/receipt → invoice matching → exception follow-up → approval/payment. The review sits after invoice arrival; local handoffs remain unverified. HMRC describes matching and legitimate non-PO purchases. [GfC8, Purchase order; Receipt of supply; Tax invoice](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/help-with-vat-compliance-controls-guidelines-for-compliance-gfc8/procure-to-pay-part-4).

The MVP reviews reference/existence, supplier/request identity, currency and approval evidence at receipt and a cutoff for routine positive GBP invoices, preserving uncertainty and proposing human review. Exclusions: emergency, capital, PO-exempt and credit documents. Remaining PO value, line matching, receipts, disputes, tax and payment decisions are outside scope. Passing header checks does not establish payment readiness.

KPI (eligible invoice arrivals): confirmed header exceptions ÷ (passed + confirmed exceptions), with UNKNOWN and cohort/cutoff visible. Five supports: current open confirmed exceptions by reason, their gross GBP value, median invoice age, department routing-role coverage, and arrival assessment coverage. These describe conditions, not savings.

Chosen first: a bounded review decision supporting the documented departmental handoff; operational priority remains unproven. Alternatives are advance PO setup, receipt capture/training, or improving the existing ERP report. April's management attribution to delayed annual PO setup makes prevention credible. [April report, PDF p15](https://www.buckshealthcare.nhs.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/13.2-M01-Finance-Report.pdf#page=15). Equivalent existing evidence, greater workload elsewhere, or upstream setup dominating would reverse this choice.

With no client access, SQL/CSV records, policies and roles are synthetic; BHT is not a client. Success means explainable, correct scenario decisions. Current tooling, actual burden, incremental usefulness and payment benefit remain unknown.
